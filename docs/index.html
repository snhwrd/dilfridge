01 Precooling

Bring the gas down to a few kelvin

A pulse-tube cryocooler first cools the two warmest cryogenic flanges. Copper links carry heat from the flanges to the cold head, and the flanges cool the radiation shields and intercept heat conducted down the wiring.

The incoming 3He-rich gas gives up heat at the 50 K and 4 K flanges. Below the 4 K stage, further cooling in the condensing circuit turns the gas into liquid before it reaches the counterflow exchangers.

02 Counterflow

Use the returning fluid to precool the feed

Concentrated 3He flows down the inner tube. The colder dilute solution from the mixing chamber flows toward the still through the annulus, the space between the two tubes. Heat passes from feed to return through the inner tube wall.

Many units add discrete exchangers with sintered metal at the cold end. Their large wetted surface helps overcome thermal resistance between the helium and the metal.

03 Dilution

Absorb heat across the phase boundary

Below about 0.87 K, a mixture of suitable composition separates into a lighter, 3He-rich phase above a denser, 4He-rich phase. Even near absolute zero, the dilute phase contains about 6.6 mol% 3He.

3He absorbs heat as it enters the 4He-rich liquid. Heat from the experiment flows through the mixing-chamber plate into the chamber. Pumping 3He out of the still keeps more 3He crossing the boundary to replace it.

At low temperature with the feed precooled to the chamber temperature, the ideal cooling power is approximately Q̇ ≈ 84 ṅ₃ T² in watts when T is in kelvin and the 3He molar flow ṅ₃ is in mol/s. Cooling power derivation

3He–4He phase diagram

To read the diagram, find the temperature and the overall mole fraction of 3He. Inside the shaded two-phase region, draw a horizontal tie line to the two boundaries. Its endpoints give the compositions of the two liquids at the same temperature.

At 0.50 K, a mixture with 50 mol% 3He separates into a dilute liquid with about 22 mol% 3He and a concentrated liquid with about 91 mol% 3He. Moving the overall composition along that tie line changes the amount of each liquid.

The phase boundaries meet the superfluid transition line (λ line) at the tricritical point, around 0.87 K and 67 mol% 3He. As the temperature approaches zero, the concentrated liquid approaches pure 3He and the dilute liquid still contains about 6.6 mol% 3He. That finite solubility lets dilution cooling continue at millikelvin temperatures.

04 Recovery

Evaporate 3He and return it to the inlet

The still receives the dilute solution. Near 0.7 K, 3He has a much higher vapor pressure than 4He, so pumping above the liquid removes mostly 3He. A heater helps set the evaporation and circulation rate.

The room-temperature pumps draw off the vapor, which is cleaned and returned to the condensing line. The incoming feed passes through a separate coil immersed in the still liquid. Heat passes through the coil wall into the still liquid and cools the feed before it enters the counterflow exchangers.
